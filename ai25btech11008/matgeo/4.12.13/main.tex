\textbf{Question:} \\

\vspace{0.3cm}

If the equation of the base of an equilateral triangle is $x+y=2$ and the vertex is $(2,-1)$, then find the length of the side of the triangle.
\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Answer:}

\subsection*{\textbf{Step 1: Represent the problem in vector form}}

The equation of the base of the triangle is
\begin{align}
\vec{n}^T \vec{x} = 2
\end{align}
where $\vec{n} = \begin{pmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}$ is the normal vector.  
The vertex is 
\[
\vec{A} = \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ -1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\subsection*{\textbf{Step 2: Foot of perpendicular from vertex to base}}

The distance from $\vec{A}$ to the line is obtained by projecting $\vec{A}$ onto the normal:
\begin{align}
d = \frac{\vec{n}^T \vec{A} - 2}{\|\vec{n}\|} 
= \frac{1\cdot 2 + 1\cdot (-1) - 2}{\sqrt{1^2+1^2}} 
= \frac{-1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{align}

Hence, the foot of perpendicular $\vec{D}$ is:
\begin{align}
\vec{D} = \vec{A} - d \frac{\vec{n}}{\|\vec{n}\|} 
= \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ -1\end{pmatrix} - \left(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} 
= \begin{pmatrix}2+\tfrac{1}{2} \\ -1+\tfrac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{5}{2} \\ -\tfrac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}

\subsection*{\textbf{Step 3: Length relation in equilateral triangle}}

In an equilateral triangle, the altitude length $AD$ is related to the side $a$ by:
\begin{align}
AD = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} a
\end{align}

Here $AD = \|\vec{A}-\vec{D}\|$.  

\[
AD = \sqrt{\left(2-\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^2 + \left(-1+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2}
= \sqrt{\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2}
= \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{4}} 
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\]

\subsection*{\textbf{Step 4: Solve for side length}}

From (3):
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} a 
\implies a = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{3}} 
= \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
\end{align}

\subsection*{Final Answer:}
\[
\boxed{a = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}}
\]
